# Business Protection v3 — Implementation Status & Gap Report

_Date: 2026-06-07 · Read-only verification against `docs/audit-v3` and `docs/business-protection-v3`. Every claim below is traced to actual code/content; policy-page-exists is distinguished from behavior-is-true throughout._

---

## 1. Verdict

You have already built far more of the protection layer than most pre-revenue founders ever do, and the highest-volume, most-repeated audit finding is genuinely fixed: **the pricing mismatch is closed** ($50/mo everywhere, the charged amount is resolved from a Stripe price env var, not the displayed number) and **the web clickwrap is real** — the Stripe checkout API returns HTTP 400 if `terms_accepted !== true`, so it is server-enforced, not cosmetic. The **Terms of Service is live and substantially complete** (15 sections, all four products, CA governing law, liability cap), and the whole policy suite (Terms, AUP, AI Output, Privacy, Subprocessors, Deletion/Export, Recording) exists and renders. The ShipMind Chrome-extension privacy policy is accurate to the code. **Do not rebuild any of that.**

What still needs attention is narrower but sharp, and clusters into three themes: (a) **promises that aren't backed by behavior** — the Critical one is deletion/export, which is a manual-email promise with *zero* working code path, plus desktop "delete" actions that orphan the most sensitive files on disk; (b) **the desktop apps accept nothing and consent to nothing in-app** — all acceptance lives on the website, while every high-privilege capability (shell agents, mic recording, cloud egress) lives in the apps; and (c) **two factual-accuracy problems that are FTC/legal exposure**: marketing still says ShipMind "never sends the underlying documents" (the RAG code sends exactly that) and ShipTalk's unconditional "on-device / no internet required" headlines coexist with cloud STT engines — the Rust source even self-flags the contradiction. Two licensing landmines remain: **GPL ffmpeg is still bundled and executed** in the paid signed ShipMind (a Critical copyleft breach), and **no OSS NOTICE/attribution ships anywhere**. None of these require an LLC to fix; most are content edits and one auditable manual SOP.

**The few things that actually need to happen before the next paid release or PDF distribution:** (1) make deletion/export real, even if only a logged manual runbook; (2) resolve GPL ffmpeg + ship a NOTICE file; (3) fix the false ShipMind "documents never leave" and ShipTalk "on-device" claims; (4) add a desktop first-run "I Agree" gate; (5) add DeepSeek (China transfer) to the subprocessor list and remove phantom Sentry.

## 4. FINISH THESE (Partial — ordered by protection-per-effort)

These pages/flows exist and work; they need content or one concrete addition. Highest protection-for-least-effort first.

1. **AUP — add four named clauses (one file, content-only).** The page is a working, footer-linked, ToS-incorporated skeleton — expand, don't rebuild. Add: (a) a ShipSpace shell-agent clause ("not a sandbox; you assume responsibility for everything autonomous agents do, especially in auto-approve/bypass modes"); (b) an MCP anti-exfiltration clause (no pointing untrusted agents at the local MCP endpoints to reach another party's corpus/session token); (c) an express no-circumvention/anti-tier-spoofing clause; (d) a third-party/provider-ToS clause covering yt-dlp/scraping **and** BYO-key provider billing. Mirror the no-resale term and cross-link the Recording Policy. Evidence: `acceptable-use/page.tsx:13-27`; gaps per `05-policy-suite.md:153-162`, `03-liability-review.md:147`.
2. **AI Output Policy — add the agent/injection section it was supposed to have.** The page renders and the generic "AI can be wrong" disclaimers are sound, but it entirely omits the risk class this product actually has: prompt injection from untrusted content (GitHub issues, web pages, MCP/bound notes) and autonomous agent actions on the user's machine. Add binding waiver/assumption-of-risk language (not advisory "should review"), make human-in-the-loop a risk-shift for users who enable auto-approval/acceptEdits, and add the missing pillars 5 (output-ownership/non-infringement responsibility) and 6 (no exclusivity). Evidence: `ai-output-policy/page.tsx:1-33` (grep for inject/waive/autonom = 0 hits); bar in `02-legal-agreements.md:167-173`.
3. **Refund/subscription Terms — add three short clauses + a real cancel path.** Content (low effort): add a comp-access "discretionary and revocable at any time" clause, an EU/UK 14-day withdrawal carve-out alongside no-refund, and a BYO-key cost-responsibility sentence. The harder one: the Terms promise cancel "through the available account or billing support flow" but **no cancel route or billing portal exists in code** (`api/` has only checkout/status/webhook/merch-checkout; no `account/billing`). FTC ROSCA / CA ARL require cancellation as easy as signup — either wire a Stripe customer-portal link or, minimally, give a concrete cancellation contact and align the Terms wording to what actually exists. Evidence: `terms/page.tsx:53-105`; `03-liability-review.md:188`, `05-tos-recommendations.md:214-218`.
4. **ToS — close the two missing required clauses.** (a) BYO-key billing responsibility: state plainly that AI usage on the user's own keys is billed by the provider, is the user's sole responsibility, and is not part of the $50 membership (the one flatly-missing checklist item; drives chargebacks). (b) Strengthen §11 to enumerate the destructive/agent-specific risks (agent file deletion/corruption, keystroke mis-paste, incomplete deletion) and add a "you must back up; we are not a backup service" clause. Optional via counsel: arbitration/class-waiver, indemnification. Evidence: `terms/page.tsx:53-64,151-156`; `05-tos-recommendations.md:99-117,200-218`.
5. **Web clickwrap — make it provable, not just present.** Today it captures only a boolean (+ timestamp at signup); no document version or content hash is stored, and the checkout acceptance lives only in Stripe metadata that the webhook never reads back into your DB. Persist (document title+version+hash, UTC timestamp, user/installation id, method) into an acceptance log you own, for both signup and checkout. A boolean is not enforceable evidence. Evidence: `signup/page.tsx:49-63`, `webhook/route.ts:99-132`; `09-user-responsibility-controls.md` URC-9.
6. **Recording & consent — add a one-time in-app acknowledgment at first mic use.** The website policy page exists and is footer-linked, but no app shows any consent framing before `getUserMedia`/mic engages (ShipTalk onboarding + Permissions request OS permission only; ShipMind `useVoiceLoop.ts:94`; ShipSpace `RightRail.tsx:1453`). Add a first-mic-use consent acknowledgment in all three (prioritize ShipTalk) and persist it. Until then two-party-consent liability sits with you personally. Evidence: `recording-capture-policy/page.tsx`; grep for consent/two-party/wiretap = 0 hits outside the policy page; `09-user-responsibility-controls.md:108`.

## 5. STILL OPEN (Missing / Broken — Critical & High)

Nothing remediating has been built for these since the audit. Verified by git history + working-tree inspection.

### CRITICAL

- **Deletion & export does not work — the promise is unbacked.** `/deletion-export` and `/privacy` promise Access / Export (JSON) / Delete, but the only path is a manual email to `privacy@`. Repo-wide search of `app/api`, `supabase/migrations`, and `scripts` found **no** deletion endpoint, no `auth.admin.deleteUser`, no JSON export route. `scripts/comp-access.mjs` only flips `subscription_tier`. In the apps the audit defects are unchanged: ShipTalk's delete button never renders (`App.tsx:264` omits `onDeleteItem`); ShipMind's `delete_transcript` (`lib.rs:3294`) and `delete_source` (`lib.rs:4049`) delete only the DB row, **orphaning raw voice audio and copied source images on disk forever** — the most sensitive data is the data that survives a "delete." _Minimum acceptable cure now: a written, logged DSAR runbook (verify identity, exact tables/Stripe/Printful records, ticket per request, the 30-day clock the policy already commits to). A reliably-executed logged manual process is legally sufficient for a single operator and needs no code._ This is the single highest-severity gap in the suite.
- **GPL ffmpeg is bundled AND executed in the paid signed ShipMind.** `tauri.conf.json:50-54` declares the `binaries/ffmpeg` sidecar; the aarch64 binary's banner shows `--enable-gpl ... license: GPL version 2 or later`. `find_binary()` prefers the bundled sidecar *first* (`lib.rs:790-808`) before the PATH fallback (`:829-845`), so in a signed release the GPL build is both conveyed and run — with no GPL text, no corresponding-source offer, no disclosure. Each distribution is a copyleft breach. Pick one: stop bundling (rely on PATH), swap to a non-GPL/LGPL build (drop `--enable-gpl`/x264/x265/vidstab/kvazaar), or fully comply (ship source + GPL text). **Record the chosen decision in writing in this folder so it is auditable.** This is the top legal blocker before further paid ShipMind sales.

### HIGH

- **No OSS attribution ships anywhere.** Repo-wide search for NOTICE / THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES / attribution / credits across all three apps = 0 results; no in-app "Open Source Licenses" screen; no `cargo-deny`/`cargo-about` tooling. Hundreds of MIT/Apache/BSD/ISC deps + MPL-2.0 crates + bundled native binaries (whisper.cpp, deno/V8/ICU, ollama/ggml, yt-dlp) + SIL-OFL Geist font all require their notices to travel with the binary. This cannot be papered over by Terms — you must generate and ship a NOTICE bundle and surface it in-app.
- **Desktop apps accept and consent to nothing in-app.** No EULA/AUP "I Agree" gate on first run in ShipSpace, ShipTalk, or ShipMind; grep for terms-version/eulaVersion/accepted_at across all three = 0 matches. ShipTalk's "onboarding" is a mic/accessibility/hotkey permission wizard with a Skip button and no terms text (`Onboarding.tsx`). ShipSpace renders straight into the workspace; its high-privilege auto-answer escalation has advisory copy only ("Use only when you trust the assignment", `SettingsModal.tsx:555-571`) and no consent record. ShipTalk's Cloud Features toggle is a bare boolean (`cloudFeatures.ts:8-16`) — no affirmative acceptance, no sub-processor list, no DeepSeek/cross-border flag. **The high-privilege capability lives on the desktop, but acceptance lives only on the website** — and a user who installs without the web signup path accepts nothing. Build a first-run scrollable "I Agree" gate (EULA+AUP) in all three, a distinct ShipSpace consent gate before agents get shell/file/exec capability, and persist acceptance evidence (version+hash, timestamp, install id, method) — without the record the gates are unprovable.
- **Marketing: ShipMind "never sends the underlying documents" is FALSE.** Copy says "only the prompt text leaves the machine, never the underlying documents" (`docs/shipmind-product-copy.md:52,54`) and "the cloud provider only sees the specific query you send, never your whole library" (`v3/shipmind-v2/page.tsx:489`). The RAG code sends exactly the opposite: `search_sources` returns retrieved chunk `text` and `read_source` returns up to MAX_SOURCE_READ_CHARS of source content into the prompt (`agentChatStore.ts:78-108`), and `callCloudVision` sends the base64 image itself to the provider (`vision.ts:59,74,81-90`). Scope the "Never leave"/"never escape" visuals to local-only mode and state the true behavior for the cloud path. FTC §5 + breach-of-warranty exposure aimed at the highest-stakes users.
- **Marketing: ShipTalk unconditional "on-device / no internet required" coexists with cloud engines — self-flagged in the source.** `page.tsx:78,2799` and `products/shiptalk/v3/page.tsx:1557` make unconditional on-device claims, while two cloud STT engines (`fileTranscribe.ts:29,48,57`) and the WebKit "Browser (Instant)" engine (`SettingsView.tsx:94`) route audio off-device. The Rust source itself carries the comment "This contradicts the '100% on-device' marketing claim" (`Cargo.toml:29`) — **still unresolved.** Also remove "fastest law-tech/legal teams ship on" (`SpeedBand.tsx:109`) unless substantiable, and retire the two stale repo-root `ShipMind_Private_*.pdf` files which predate the hedged copy.
- **Privacy/subprocessor list is both over- and under-inclusive.** Under-inclusive (most serious): **DeepSeek** is a fully wired chat provider that forwards user prompts to `api.deepseek.com` (China) with a platform key (`api/chat/deepseek/route.ts:15`) but is **not named** anywhere, and there is no international-transfer/residency/opt-out notice. Over-inclusive: **Sentry** is listed as an active error-reporting subprocessor but no `@sentry` SDK is installed and nothing sends to it — only a CSP host allowance (`privacy:34,61`, `next.config.ts:21`); it's a phantom promise. Groq/OpenRouter are listed as website providers but are desktop-BYOK-only. And the pages frame website AI as "BYO governed by their terms" when the platform actually holds the keys and forwards content (you are the controller). Add DeepSeek + transfer notice, remove Sentry until wired, separate website-server-side subprocessors from desktop-BYOK, and add client IP to "What We Collect." The extension policy is accurate — leave it.

## 6. LLC-Deferred Posture — what stays personally exposed

Deferring the LLC until revenue is a defensible call, and **most items above are fixable without an entity** — they are content edits, one manual SOP, a NOTICE file, and in-app gates. State this honestly: an LLC does not fix any of §4 or §5; those are real work regardless. What the missing entity *does* leave on the table is the **liability shield itself**. Specifically:

- **Personal liability is unavoidable without an entity.** Every contract (ToS), every liability cap, and every AUP allocation is between the user and *you personally*. The 12-month-fees cap in `terms/page.tsx:151-156` caps the company's exposure — but with no company, that is your personal exposure capped, and a court can disregard caps more readily against an individual. A deceptive-claims action (the §5 marketing items) or a GPL enforcement action (the ffmpeg item) lands on you directly.
- **The caps and AUP are only as strong as the acceptance behind them.** Because acceptance is a boolean with no version/hash (§4.5) and the desktop apps capture nothing (§5), the very instruments meant to limit personal liability are weakly enforceable today. Fixing acceptance evidence is the cheapest way to harden the personal-liability position *before* the LLC exists.
- **Honest bottom line:** the LLC is correctly deferred, but two things should not wait for it because they are personal-exposure-now, entity-or-not — (1) stop the false marketing claims and the GPL breach (these create liability that no cap helps), and (2) make deletion/export real and capture provable acceptance (these make the protections you've already built actually hold). Once revenue justifies the entity, re-paper the ToS/AUP under the LLC name and add the entity-level items (insurance per blueprint 07, governance per 08).
