# Corporate Protection Checklist

Status: Internal operating control.

Purpose: keep business liability, assets, contracts, payments, and records separated from Jake personally.

## Current Operating Posture

MakeShipHappen is currently personal/founder-operated and intended to transition into a formal business after revenue proves the business. The immediate goal is to preserve clean records, avoid misleading claims, use business-facing contact channels, and prepare for entity formation without incurring entity costs too early.

## Recommended Next Corporate Move

Do not form the LLC immediately. Revisit entity formation once MakeShipHappen reliably clears approximately $5,000-$8,000/month from the website, or earlier if B2B/team customers, contractors, investors, enterprise contracts, lifetime access, or higher-liability use cases accelerate the risk profile. When the trigger is reached, decide whether the first formal entity should be an LLC or corporation with a qualified attorney/CPA.

## Pre-LLC Operating Controls

- [ ] Track monthly gross revenue, net revenue, Stripe fees, refunds, chargebacks, and platform costs.
- [ ] Keep business income and expenses categorized even if still paid personally.
- [ ] Save Stripe receipts, customer communications, cancellation records, and support messages.
- [ ] Use role email forwarders for customer-facing communication.
- [ ] Keep public pricing, refund, product-status, and privacy claims consistent.
- [ ] Avoid B2B, enterprise, regulated-data, or compliance claims without attorney review.
- [ ] Revisit LLC/entity formation at approximately $5,000-$8,000/month in reliable website revenue.
- [ ] Revisit sooner before selling lifetime access at scale.

- [ ] MFA enabled on critical accounts.
- [ ] Recovery codes stored securely.
- [ ] Emergency access process documented.

- [ ] Signing certificates and release keys inventoried.

## Contract Hygiene

- [ ] Terms of Service under legal entity name.
- [ ] Privacy Policy under legal entity name.
- [ ] Stripe statement descriptor matches brand/entity.
- [ ] Customer receipts identify entity.
- [ ] Support emails use domain email.
- [ ] Contractor/freelancer agreements assign IP to entity.
- [ ] Any advisor/investor paperwork stored centrally.

## Founder Liability Rules

1. Sign contracts as the entity, not personally, unless unavoidable.
2. Keep business funds separate.
3. Use company email for business commitments.
4. Avoid informal promises that contradict Terms.
5. Avoid guaranteeing outcomes, revenue, security, privacy, uptime, legal compliance, or AI accuracy.
6. Route legal/privacy/security complaints into documented processes.
